Questions to ask before you hire the best landscaping company grovetown Harlem offers

You don't need to be an expert. You just need a few straight questions and a good ear for the answers.

  • What work do you do yourselves, and what do you subcontract?
  • Do you handle both lawn care and hardscaping, or only one?
  • How do you price a job, and what does the estimate cover?
  • What does the schedule look like, and what happens if rain pushes it back?
  • Who do I call if something looks wrong after the work is done?

A company that answers clearly and doesn't rush you is usually a company that runs the job the same way.

A short checklist for comparing landscapers

Put two or three companies side by side and check these things.

  • They ask about your yard, your soil, your drainage and how you use the space.
  • They walk the property with you instead of quoting from a phone call.
  • The estimate is written down and spells out what's included.
  • They can show you finished work similar to what you want.
  • They answer the phone and call back when they say they will.

If a company ticks most of those, you're in decent shape. If they dodge the basics, move on.

Mistakes to avoid when choosing a landscaping company

The cheapest number is the most common trap. A low quote that leaves out grading, base material or cleanup often grows once the work starts.

Another one is skipping the drainage conversation. In this part of Georgia, heavy rain can wreck a new patio or fresh sod if water has nowhere to go. Ask about it up front.

People also hire a mowing-only crew for a hardscape job, or the other way around. Mowing, sod and aeration are one skill set. Retaining walls, paver walkways and fire pits are another. It helps when one company does both and plans them together.

Last, don't leave the plan in your head. If you want a fire pit near the patio, say so now, not after the pavers are down.

What to prepare before the first visit

A few minutes of prep saves everyone time.

  • Walk your yard and note what bugs you most.
  • Take a few photos, including problem spots after rain.
  • Think about how you'll use the space: kids, pets, cookouts, quiet evenings.
  • Know where your utility lines, sprinklers and property lines are, if you can.
  • Have a rough idea of your budget and timing, even if it's a range.

When to call a pro

Some jobs are fine for a weekend. Others aren't. Call a pro when you're dealing with a slope that's moving, a lawn that needs sod or aeration done right, or any retaining wall, paver patio, walkway or fire pit. Those jobs depend on the base underneath, and mistakes show up a year later.
