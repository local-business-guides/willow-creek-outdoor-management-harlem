Questions to ask a Grovetown landscaping company before you hire

You don't need to be an expert. You just need a few straight answers.

  • What exactly is included in the quote? Ask whether it covers prep work, materials, cleanup and hauling away old material.
  • Who will be on my property, and will someone walk the site with me first?
  • How do you handle drainage? This matters for patios, walkways and retaining walls.
  • What does the schedule look like, and what happens if weather pushes it back?
  • Do you do both lawn work and hardscaping, or will I need two companies?

A company that answers these without dodging is a good sign. If the answers are vague, keep looking.

A short checklist for comparing quotes

When you've talked to two or three companies, line the quotes up side by side.

  • Is the scope written down, not just talked about?
  • Are materials named (type of paver, type of sod, wall block)?
  • Does it say who handles the base and grading for hard surfaces?
  • Is there a plain start window and a rough finish time?
  • Did the person who quoted actually see your yard?

The cheapest number isn't always the best one. A low quote that skips base prep on a paver patio can cost you more when it settles and shifts.

Mistakes to avoid when choosing a landscaping company in Grovetown

A few things trip people up again and again.

Ordering sod before the ground is ready. Fresh sod needs a graded, cleared surface. Laying it over weeds and bumps just buries the problem.

Skipping aeration on a tired lawn. If the soil is packed down, new seed or fertilizer won't do much. Georgia clay compacts easily.

Designing a patio with no thought for water. Where the rain goes matters as much as how the pavers look.

Hiring for a big job without a site visit. Photos help, but a real walk-through catches things like slope, roots and access.

What to have ready before you call

A little prep speeds up the estimate. Have a rough idea of what you want, a few photos of the area, and a sense of your timeline. If you know roughly how big the space is, say so. If you have a budget in mind, share it. A good crew can tell you what's realistic and what to phase over time.

If you're in a neighborhood with an HOA, check its rules on walls, fire pits and fencing before you commit to a design.

When to call a pro

Mowing and basic upkeep are fine to do yourself if you enjoy it. Call a pro when the job needs the right equipment, a solid base or real know-how. That usually means sod installation, aeration, retaining walls, paver patios and walkways, and fire pits.
