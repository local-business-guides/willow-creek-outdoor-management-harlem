Questions to ask a top rated landscaping company Grovetown Harlem homeowners call

A good landscaper won't mind questions. If someone gets short with you before you've hired them, that's worth noting.

  • What exactly is included in the quote, and what isn't?
  • Who will be on my property, and how long do you expect the job to take?
  • How do you handle weather delays? Georgia rain can push a schedule around.
  • What happens to old sod, soil or leftover material?
  • Is the work done in-house or handed off to someone else?

Write the answers down. You'll want them if details get fuzzy later.

A short checklist before you say yes

Run through this before you sign or shake on anything:

1. Get the scope in writing, with the areas and materials named.
2. Ask to see photos of similar projects, like a patio, a retaining wall or a new lawn.
3. Confirm who to call if something looks off after the work is done.
4. Make sure the start date and the rough finish date are both on paper.
5. Ask how drainage will be handled, especially on sloped lots.

That last one gets skipped a lot. A pretty patio that floods your back door isn't a win.

Mistakes to avoid when choosing a landscaper

The lowest bid is rarely the best deal. If one quote is far under the others, ask what's missing. Often it's base prep, and base prep is what keeps pavers and walls from shifting.

Another common slip is hiring before you've decided what you want. Without a plan, the scope grows halfway through and so does the bill. Spend an evening walking your yard and listing the problems you want solved.

Also, don't put sod down in the wrong season or skip soil work just to hurry. Grass planted on poor ground struggles no matter how good it looks on day one.

What to prepare before the first visit

A little prep saves everyone time.

  • Know your property lines and any HOA rules on fences, walls or outdoor features.
  • Mark sprinkler heads, buried lines and anything else underground you know about.
  • Make a rough budget range so the conversation stays realistic.
  • Take a few phone photos of the problem spots, like bare patches, standing water or slopes.
  • Think about how you actually use the yard. Kids, dogs, cookouts and shade all change the design.

When to call a pro

Small mowing and trimming jobs are fine to do yourself if you enjoy it. Call a pro when the work involves heavy materials, grading, or a result that has to last. That means retaining walls, paver patios and walkways, fire pits, sod installation and lawn aeration on a larger yard. Ongoing lawn maintenance is another good one to hand off, so weekends stay yours.
